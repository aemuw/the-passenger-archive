# Ideas — after re-reading the whole concept

## What the concept is really about
Not "a train with monsters" but **an anthology of memory with one hidden story inside it**: every carriage teaches the player one thing
(*you cannot change what happened; you can notice*), and the last carriage turns it on the player. The strongest moments so far are the small ones:
a phone that kills in carriage 1 and *rings again at the terminus*; a stranger who is kind in every carriage; a name ("Максим") you may or may not
have asked for, appearing later in your own phone. Build on **recognition**, not on jump scares.

## Added in v3 (done)
- Hero's reason: ad for Marta's late father's camera → night, cash, alone, "only tonight"; Marta's voicemail names each red flag.
- His killer is the "seller" Максим, who rode along to make sure the hero came alone. Name can be asked in all three carriages.
- Everyone has a voice (Viktor — apologetic accountant; Zakhar — flat; Olya — nervous chatter; Halyna — clipped irony; Solomiya — polite, apologises;
  Hanna — gossip; Bohdan — defensive; Levko — brusque, lies; Taras — too kind; Maksym — jokey) and a name you can ask for.
- Living NPCs: breathing, nodding/gesturing while talking, glancing at windows, fidgeting, sleeping, muttering when you linger.
- Look: surface textures (wood, fabric, plaster, metal, lino), hair/hands/shoes/glasses/beards/bags, lamp haze, dust, straps, curtains, posters, VHS bleed/glitch.
- Sound: footsteps, creaks, thuds, PA chime, wind, rain, heartbeat, one five-note motif (piano / echo / crushed), drone pad.

## Next, by value
1. **The ticket becomes the archive** (the title!). Each finished carriage punches a hole and adds a card: story, FICTIONAL/INSPIRED/DOCUMENTED,
   and a "Real-world context" page you can re-read after the credits. This is where the social purpose lives — not in forced rules.
2. **"What did you notice?" recap** at the end of a carriage (3 gentle choices, no score): the signs that *other people* could have noticed.
   Keeps the lesson on the community, never on the victim.
3. **Victims keep riding.** Viktor sits in the back of carriage 2, Solomiya in 3 — silent, looking at the window. A nod when you pass.
   Killers also stay in the carriage you leave (the camera lingers a second before the card): "the train kept going".
4. **Window reflections** (fake): the glass shows passengers a beat late, or the man who is not in his seat. Reflection of Максим sitting where nobody is.
5. **Second run changes**: his gestures get a second tell; the phone messages in the depart memory show his earlier texts; the conductor answers a new question.
6. **The conductor's three answers** return in the last carriage with new meaning ("Далі." / "Пасажири." / "А чому ти?") — he never lies, he just answers a different question.
7. **Content/resources screen** at the start and after the credits (content warning; links to real support lines — verify the current numbers before shipping).
8. **Autosave per carriage** and an accessibility pass (text size, reduce glitch/flash, subtitles for sounds).
9. **A DOCUMENTED carriage without a villain** (carbon monoxide) — see REAL_STORIES.md — to break the "somebody did this" pattern.
10. **Nightmare carriages** for rhythm (no victim, no moral): the Window carriage (same house for 10 minutes), the Echo carriage ("Не відповідай").

## Risks to keep an eye on
- Victim-blaming (see REAL_STORIES.md). Keep "rules" for fictional stories; for real ones put the burden on bystanders/institutions.
- The "hero ignored Marta" arc must read as *a human mistake planted by someone else's trap*, not as "he deserved it" (the ending card says so on purpose).
- Violence is shown on purpose; keep it brief and consequential, never lingering.
